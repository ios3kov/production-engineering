# Research Methodology

Use for research questions where a quick fact lookup is insufficient. Choose depth proportionate to the question and the cost of error.

## Research cycle

1. **Scope.** Define the question, decision it supports, geography, time period, definitions, success criteria and evidence gaps.
2. **Plan sources.** Seek official/primary documents first, then independent technical, academic, professional and community sources where appropriate. Note incentive conflicts and publication dates.
3. **Gather.** Retrieve directly; distinguish full-text inspection from search snippets or abstracts. Track the source URL or document ID, publisher, publication/revision date and access date.
4. **Evaluate.** Rate relevance, independence, methodological quality and freshness; actively seek counterexamples and contradictory explanations.
5. **Analyze.** Separate known facts, estimates and judgment. Reconcile incompatible figures through definitions and denominators rather than averaging them blindly.
6. **Synthesize.** Give the bottom line, strongest evidence, key alternatives, limitations and actionable next step. Cite each material factual claim near its statement.
7. **Preserve when needed.** For substantial reusable projects, create an evidence log or report in the user's requested destination. Do not silently save to external services.

## Rigor

Do not fill gaps with plausible numbers, invented quotes, fabricated citations or assumptions presented as observations. Treat inaccessible, paywalled or missing source material as unverified. In fast-moving subjects verify the relevant current timeframe, and indicate the cutoff.

A one-minute lookup should remain a one-minute lookup; do not force a full research dossier for an ordinary question.
